## Update (June 2020)

My TOEFL result came out. It was R:16, L:22, S:16, W:22, total 76. If I take it one more time, maybe I can totally squeeze out 4 more points...?

## Why I Took It

When applying to a certain graduate school entrance exam at the end of August, I was asked to submit one score from IELTS/TOEFL iBT/TOEFL iPT/TOEIC L&R.

I originally wanted to submit TOEIC since it's easiest for me to score on, but:

- Due to COVID, I couldn't take TOEIC for a while
- I hadn't taken TOEIC for around 3 years

Because of that, I decided to take TOEFL.

## Test Day Situation

From the morning, I cleaned my room and prepared my desk (with light), laptop, passport, and whiteboard (with eraser and pen).

## Reading

Total disaster.

In the materials I'd been using, Reading was usually either my best of the 4 sections or at least fairly good, so from around the previous week I _didn't study Reading at all_, thinking it'd be fine somehow.

The biggest problem was I didn't know the time per Reading passage had changed to 18 minutes, so I kept reading at a 20-minute pace. Also:

1. Recently I hadn't read long passages much, so my reading speed got slower
2. I got slower at finding keywords in paragraphs
3. A ton of unknown vocabulary appeared
4. **Lost concentration**
5. **Outside environmental noise distracted me a lot**

Especially points 1, 2, and 3 were caused by "my lack of ability" + "overconfidence."

For point 4, I think my training method so far was bad. Usually when solving **Reading practice, I solved one passage at a time, or at most two consecutively**, so that's likely the cause.

Because of this, I somehow solved the first two passages, but from the third onward there were many moments where my concentration dropped repeatedly. I need to improve how I study.

## Listening

Listening was still on-and-off in concentration, but I managed to catch enough somehow.

However, one of the lecture-type question sets was insanely difficult.

**I repeatedly experienced a state where, even after hearing the audio, I couldn't recall the corresponding word** (more than usual).

So I ended up like "I understand the flow of the talk, but I can't explain how the hypothesis was proposed and what exactly it was..." My ears not being used to it is part of it, but this is **completely a vocabulary shortage**.

The score shown right after the test felt fairly convincing (not bad, actually good).

## Speaking

For Speaking, for better or worse I spoke pretty much as usual.

But I handled my weak point, **finishing within the time limit**, with full awareness this time, so it felt like a really good moment where I could sense my own growth.

My vocabulary was still weak, but I could state my opinion and even give concrete examples supporting it, which was good.

Still, since it was my first TOEFL, I truly had no idea how many points I'd get (at test end).

## Writing

Personally this was the section I was most curious about.

Actually, in practice so far, in both length and quality, I had only written answers around (probably) under 20 points, so I challenged the real test kind of with a "why not" mindset.

In practice writing, length was especially the bottleneck, and I could only write _about 400 words_ on average. On top of that, the balance was poor, like only the conclusion paragraph being conspicuously short among the four paragraphs.

But for some reason it went well in the real test, and I ended up with a very satisfying answer of _about 465 words_. A rare case where I could do in the real thing what I couldn't do in practice.

If I had to be greedy, I'd like around 23 points, but honestly I have no idea...
