There were two reasons I decided to read them.

1. I recently witnessed someone going absolutely ballistic nearby
2. I wanted to live life on easy mode

Well, point 1 isn't that important. But as everyone knows, **if you lose your temper and say something inappropriate at work, you're socially dead on the spot**.

I decided I wanted to make sure I never blow my top, and ideally spread anger management skills throughout the workplace (though it would've been nice if there were company training for this in the first place… but that's a separate story).

For me personally, point 2 is actually more important. My philosophy in life is to cut corners wherever I can, and to work hard _in order_ to be able to cut corners. I also believe that the more allies you have, the better. So I started thinking: "If I can live life on even easier mode, isn't anger management the ultimate bang-for-your-buck skill?"

## My Past Self, and What I Learned from the Books

Honestly, in the past, when I had to deal with technologies or projects at work that I didn't want to handle, my feelings (of "ugh, this is such a drag…") would show on my face.

That's because I had **assumed** that "this job or technology has no direct connection to living an easier life in the future."

And back in university, I sometimes felt a twinge of irritation toward labmates and juniors who were often scolded by the professor — thinking, "Why don't they just get [X] done already?"

That was also because I had **assumed** that "regardless of any background, everyone has to try their best not to get scolded by the supervisor" (though I still think students should make an effort, of course).

On the other hand, reading the books made me realize just how important it is to reflect on past moments when I felt anger (even small ones) — it's a crucial practice for understanding your own core values and beliefs.

I've only just started categorizing my own anger and stress responses, and I plan to continue reflecting regularly to better understand and refine my core awareness with higher resolution.

### On Controlling Impulses

At the same time, I felt that I had already been practicing "impulse control" naturally.

"Magic words," "grounding," and "timeout" were all techniques I'd been doing naturally before reading the books. They had become instinctive from past experiences as a way to protect myself from danger (I had learned to choose to walk away rather than snap back when confronted).

Seeing these methods introduced as legitimate, approved techniques gives me the confidence to keep using them! ^^♪

## Afterthought

I imagine that employees at large corporations carrying major responsibilities, or those at relatively comfortable companies, learn this kind of thing through compliance training.

On the other hand, at small companies running on a shoestring, training like anger management inevitably ends up being a very low priority.

That said, the importance of preventing compliance violations and harassment is increasingly understood regardless of company size, and I think standards will only get stricter going forward.

Anyway, what I'm trying to say is: for modern working adults, anger management is probably the highest bang-for-your-buck, must-have subject for unlocking easy mode in life (subjective opinion).

Happy anger management!
