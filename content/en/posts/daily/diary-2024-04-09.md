## Surprises from Year 1

The biggest surprise from working life is that, for better or worse, this world runs on a lot of improvisation.

Take programming, since that's my field: there's legacy and outright dangerous code that people use without really understanding what's inside, and software where the algorithm isn't even documented (lol). Oh, and of course not a single line of tests.

And yet, people before us built and delivered all sorts of services and products on top of that — or rather, somehow managed to pull it off.

Every time I see something that exists in such a miraculous balance, I think "man, this world actually runs on a lot of guesswork." At the same time, it made me want to loosen up a bit myself[^1].

There were a few other surprises too:

- Surprisingly, most people (sweeping generalization) don't have deep interest in the work they do or the tools they use
- Very few people understand the importance of sharing what they know with everyone in order to improve overall team efficiency

The above is specific to my workplace, and I don't think it generalizes to every workplace. But there were very few people who proactively shared the kind of tips and how-to's that everyone could benefit from in their work.

If you think about it logically, to achieve the goal of "getting work done efficiently," sharing "technical tips common to everyone's work" — basically the fundamental principles of work and coding — seems obviously useful, right? But surprisingly few people did it...

"If you have time for that, just get your own work done" is a comeback you might hear, but from a long-term perspective and from the perspective of cultivating company culture, isn't sharing knowledge clearly better? I think so.

Even if it's knowledge that only applies in personally limited situations, sharing it is always better than not. After all, whether to use the information is up to the receiver, so even "garbage" information is better to have than not (within reason, of course).

And beyond that, I also feel that whether there's a place where people can casually share information matters more than hierarchy — and my workplace has almost none of that.

"Then just create it!" you might think. If I could do that, I probably wouldn't be writing about this here...

## Joys

For the time being, I'm happy to have worked a full year without getting sick, stayed mentally stable, and never missed a day.

Especially since at our company, you get zero paid days off for the first six months of year one, so if you get the flu, you're automatically out five days without pay... (not a black company per se, and it's all legally compliant, but still)

If I had to add something else, I was happy to have passed the Computational Solid Mechanics Level 2 exam. Thanks to passing it, my salary nearly doubled. Personally it's not a satisfying amount, but specifically, including the base raise, my salary went up about ¥26,600.

Not related to work, but completing my first half-marathon was also something I was happy about.

## Sorrows

Working at my current company feels not much different from solo development.

The differences from solo development are:

- Specs for what to build are decided to some extent upfront
- You get paid
- You can build it without any sense of ownership

That's about it. Also, for tools, languages, and design knowledge, I basically google it myself or ask ChatGPT, so I almost never ask colleagues anything... The only things I ask about (or rather report on) are work approach, estimates, and progress.

With all that, I can't help but feel "does it have to be this company?" very strongly.

- Proper training
- Genuine OJT
- A career model worth aspiring to

My workplace has none of the above, so the feeling of "I don't need to be at this specific company" is strong. And as I mentioned, "ownership" is what I feel matters most to doing interesting work, and at my current job it's not required — in fact, having less of it might actually make things move faster — which is pretty tough.

Plus, at this workplace, you build the skill to get work done smoothly, but when you go out into the world, you'd just be another person who can write some code...

## Going Forward

I've worked a year for the time being, but basically I feel like I'm in lukewarm water, not much different from part-time work, so I'm thinking of moving to a different environment by the end of this fiscal year.

What I'm looking for in the next environment (some of which my current job does partially have):

- Salary (important. More money literally expands your options in life.)
- Psychological safety (honestly hard to verify from the outside)
  - Could also be restated as: a team where you can talk about both work and nonsense easily
  - A workplace/team where you can have healthy disagreements
- Being able to keep working on projects where I can master the technology
- Being involved in building and improving something good with genuine ownership
- Being involved in projects where we build something as a team
- (Ideally) A workplace where I can work with engineers etc. from overseas

[^1]: The word _適当_ here means exactly fitting the required standard — not above, not below.
