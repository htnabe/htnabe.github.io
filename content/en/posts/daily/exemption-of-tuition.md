Below, I have documented information about the system I utilized and my personal financial situation. Please note that the information provided here is for master's degree (doctoral first-stage) students and is current as of January 2023.

## Which System Did You Use?

System Used: Tuition Waiver Based on University Policy

This is the "② Exemption based on Shimane University policy (for economic hardship and other special circumstances)" introduced on the page below.

{{<blogcard url="https://www.shimane-u.ac.jp/procedure/tuition_waiver/tuition_waiver02.html">}}

### Standards Set by "Shimane University Policy"

To receive tuition waiver under this system, you must meet both academic and household income criteria.

- **Academic Criteria (for first-year graduate students):** Completion of at least 110 credits by the end of the fourth undergraduate year with an academic achievement index of 3.0 or higher
- **Household Income Criteria:** As shown in the table of income standards below

Please note the following three points. For information not listed here, please refer to the [official information](https://www.shimane-u.ac.jp/_files/00293341/2023-kijun_matsue.pdf).

> Credits that are not recognized as graduation requirements are not included.

> 'Total Income' = 'Gross Income' - [Necessary Business Expenses for Wages] - [Special Deductions]

> Figures in parentheses indicate the income standard for full waiver (may vary depending on budget circumstances)

### My Personal Situation

- Living with parents (two parents and myself)
- Receiving JASSO (Japan Student Services Organization) Type I Scholarship (¥50,000 per month)
- Parents: Retired when I became a fourth-year undergraduate
- Undergraduate GPA: Approximately 3.5 (from memory)

I will not disclose household income information, but to be honest, I didn't think I met the criteria for full exemption, so I was surprised when I first received notice of the waiver (I was so confident I didn't meet the criteria).

Regarding the academic criteria, however, I had achieved relatively high grades as an undergraduate, so I thought there would be no issue meeting that requirement.

### Application Essay

Below is the application statement I wrote. I'm including this as an example:

---

_Since last year, I have continued to be overwhelmed with research and coursework, leaving me unable to allocate time for part-time work. Additionally, although I received financial support from my parents through fiscal year 2020, since the COVID-19 pandemic began, my mother's work income has decreased and my father is unemployed, so I no longer receive support from my parents. I have been living on scholarships, depleting my savings, and income from TA work. Since I cannot expect continued financial support from my parents this year, I rely on scholarships and hope for tuition exemption._

---

## Final Thoughts

For those considering graduate school, I believe that being aware of or not aware of tuition waiver systems and scholarships (particularly grant-based or interest-free loan types), or whether you apply for them, can greatly affect your financial situation during your university years.

Although the application process can be somewhat tedious with many required documents, having nearly ¥260,000 waived in half a year is significant, isn't it? (After all, you could use that money for other things). I hope this information proves helpful to someone[^1].
