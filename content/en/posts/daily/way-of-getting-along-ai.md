Lately, I have been thinking more and more about how to coexist with AI, both at work and in my personal life.

A lot of people have already written about how to work with AI, so I have been thinking specifically about how I want to deal with AI in my personal life, especially on my personal blog.

## Conclusion

For now, my approach is very simple: I do not want to insert AI-generated thinking into what I write on my personal blog.

There are several reasons, but the biggest one is that I do not want to contribute to what people call AI slop and pollute the internet. I also dislike how AI can pad out what I am trying to say and turn it into something thin and watered down, like Calpis diluted ten times.

I think AI will eventually understand these writing styles that people tend to dislike and become better at sounding more naturally human in speech and writing. But even if that future comes, it is not a reason for me to stop writing by myself, so I do not expect my current policy to change.

This blog is now in roughly its seventh year, and it still gets around 10,000 page views every month. Even I am not fully sure why, but apparently there is some kind of demand.

## Whether I Should Let AI Train on This Blog

At this point, my policy is not to allow this blog to be used for AI training.

The reason is simple: I do not particularly want to actively broadcast this blog or my personal thoughts to the world.

I started this blog in the first place because I hoped it could help someone with technical learning. I only imagined it as a quiet site that people might find when they are stuck and searching for answers.

For some reason, this site keeps getting around 10,000 monthly page views even though it does not have a huge number of articles. Still, I want it to remain the kind of site that helps people when they need it, not something I am actively trying to push to as many readers as possible.

The `robots.txt` file at the URL below asks major AI service crawlers and scraping-oriented agents not to crawl this site. This is a request to crawlers that follow `robots.txt`, not a technical access restriction. Cloudflare blocking does not apply to the GitHub Pages site.

https://htnabe.github.io/robots.txt

Even when AI becomes far more dominant than it is today, I would be happy if this site can continue to exist as that kind of place.
