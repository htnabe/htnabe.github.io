## Background

I finished writing the parts of my master's thesis that I could write and had nothing left to do — plus, I'll likely be using a lot of English at work — so I decided to take TOEIC next month to demonstrate my English proficiency.

That said, for these past four years I haven't studied **for** the TOEIC at all, so I had plenty of anxiety about test-taking strategies and my general English ability. Can I aim for a decent score starting from this point? ~~Is there insurance I can sign up for?!~~

A diary of a small challenge.

## My English Learning History So Far

- 1st year of undergrad: TOEIC Bridge
- 2nd year of undergrad (4 years ago): TOEIC 750
- 4th year of undergrad: TOEFL iBT 76 (R:16, L:22, S:16, W:22)

As you can see, the last time I took TOEIC was four years ago, and the TOEFL I took two years after that as a milestone ended up being a somewhat underwhelming 76 points.

Also, during my master's program (years 1–2), I barely did anything resembling English study (though I did have opportunities to closely read English academic papers).

## What I'm Aiming For

I wrote the excuse-like reasons for taking TOEIC first, but separately from that, there's a version of myself I want to work toward through this opportunity.

And that's a version of myself who can consistently keep up with English training.

Just like with the TOEFL two years ago, I want to say goodbye to the version of myself who can only get motivated right before an English test. So I want to use this as an opportunity to take TOEIC regularly and keep up my English study on a regular basis.

## Where I Am Now and Going Forward

Currently I'm studying with the "TOEIC Official Practice Workbook 9" and "Kikutan Score 800" ↓. The reason I chose them: they seemed to have a good reputation. No deep reasoning behind it.

The day before yesterday, I tried solving a mock test from the official workbook under actual test time conditions (due to time constraints, I split listening and reading across two days). The results were as follows:

{{<table "table">}}
|           |                 |
| --------- | --------------- |

So — with the advantage of splitting it across two days — the result was surprisingly good. According to the workbook, that would translate to roughly 807–935 on the actual test, which I consider a decent score. But before I got too excited, I could also see some clear areas to work on:

1. Time management is unclear (especially for Reading)
2. My pre-listening skills are way too weak (especially for Listening)

The approximate **incorrect answer rate** by part is as follows — and this mock test helped me identify my weak spots.

{{<table "table">}}
|                    |        |        |        |        |        |        |        |

|                    | Part 1 | Part 2 | Part 3 | Part 4 | Part 5 | Part 6 | Part 7 |

Parts 2, 5, and 6 have incorrect rates of 15% or higher — these are my weak points. For now, here's my plan going forward:

1. Take the mock test one more time (the official workbook contains 2 full mock tests)
2. Keep up my daily vocabulary study (I'm using Quizlet right now)

Since the workbook has two mock tests and I've only done one, I plan to work through both Listening and Reading in one sitting sometime at the end of this month or early next month.

My vocabulary streak is at 7 days so far. Good job, me.
