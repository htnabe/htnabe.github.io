_There is not even 1mm of technical talk here._

I developed and released a smartphone app around Feb-Mar 2021. The app is called SUEP. It's a student app for Shimane University that collects all kinds of useful information related to the university (or at least aims to).

Originally I developed it alone, but there wasn't enough manpower for what I wanted to do, so I gathered people and switched to team development midway.

The exact release date is March 24, 2021, so to mark about one year since release, I wanted to write down various things I felt about the development process.

## Trigger

The biggest trigger for starting this app was the spread of COVID-19.

At the time, **I frequently heard from university students struggling in many ways during the endless pandemic**. On top of an already stressful new semester, university notices added restrictions like "_no activities on campus_" and "_home quarantine_ for X days or weeks when moving from outside the prefecture," and many people couldn't act freely.

At the same time, I think many people were dissatisfied with the university's response. The university asked students not to come to campus, but support matching that request was not well prepared.

Also, information from the university was posted in hard-to-access places (sites), and the whole system was not convenient for students at all (still true now).

What personally felt like a major issue then was that **information meaningful to students was scattered across too many platforms**.

Sometimes student info appeared on the university's official Twitter account, sometimes on the website, sometimes on YouTube, and so on (unfortunately, still true now).

So I started thinking: "**Let's make an app that covers Shimane University information on one platform!**"

### What Even Counts as Meaningful Information?

However, the first thing I stumbled on after starting development was: who is this app actually for?

Obviously, what people consider "important" or "meaningful" differs person to person, so I often struggled with what kind of information to focus on.

Not just requirements definition, but user targeting too. Back then I didn't know much about any of that, and I clearly remember progressing very inefficiently. From that background, I hadn't thought through enough (still true even now) **who this app is for**.

## App Development

As mentioned earlier, I was developing the app alone at release.

However, my capacity couldn't keep up with what I wanted to do, so I decided to gather people who could help with programming, design, PR, etc. (I wanted around 5 people total).

In the first ~2 months of gathering contributors, I got 2 people, then 4 more over the next 5 months. But currently, including me, only 2 members remain.

Why did this happen? Because **I couldn't fully support people who can't self-learn**.

When recruiting contributors this time, I didn't set strict conditions. So people like these came in:

- _"I have no programming experience, but I'm interested (I don't have anything specific I want to build)!"_
- _"Designer...? Nah (I have no programming experience but) I wanna do programming..."_

Of course, **I'm not dissing beginners with no programming experience**. Some people simply never had a chance before, but once they discover the fun, they dive deep on their own.

Still, looking back now, most people who came seemed to have a relatively light mindset like "programming is trendy lately, so let's poke into it."

Given that, I thought about what to do going forward and concluded that it's easy to tell who's just being trendy ~~they often come to observe with friends~~, so from now on I'll respond only to people who come alone with genuine courage.

### People Who Can Self-Learn Are Very Valuable

This is another thing I learned by trying to gather people: self-learners are valuable.

Fortunately, through a club I belonged to in undergrad, I met many people across various fields who could **self-learn**.

People who build apps on their own if left alone, people who self-study the subjects they love, people who modify what they like, etc.

I didn't realize it back then, but recently I see myself as one of those self-learners too, with a mindset that if study is needed to build something, self-learning it is natural.

Slightly changing topic, here's one example that genuinely confused me among people interested in this app development:

- When they hit an error, they wouldn't read the error message / wouldn't investigate on their own first
- They wouldn't do framework tutorials alone

What's common here is: they can't or won't try solving problems alone.

Even in tutorials for a framework you're touching for the first time, unexpected errors can happen. And during programming, you'll encounter error messages you've never seen. I've had that too.

In our team, when facing such issues, we set a rule: ask others only for problems you couldn't solve after 45+ minutes. But this means **after 45 minutes of full effort searching**, not just waiting.

Even though we set and clearly explained this development rule, I was asked multiple times to "stay with me from the start because I can't or don't want to do it alone."

If I respond to requests like that, it becomes time where neither side grows, and I personally couldn't allocate that much time, so we used a system where we accepted questions mainly for difficult problems.

**Conversely, only one person has agreed with this system and continued developing together so far.**

As this shows, people who can investigate/study and challenge problem-solving even alone are truly valuable.

## Final Thoughts

It was a good experience, but now I think development speed would probably have been faster if I stayed solo.

That said, a good point of team development is preventing knowledge/skills from becoming person-dependent. If development and operation are supported over the long term in some way, team development is probably best.

Once job hunting settles down, I plan to return to app development hard again, and lately when I have time I'm staring down books on network/server construction using AWS.
