## Current Playtime and Story Progress

At this point I've cleared the whole story and beaten the Last Sentinel on CHARISMA difficulty. Unfortunately I still haven't beaten the hidden boss on LEGEND difficulty ;;

I originally planned to review after clearing LEGEND, but it looks like it'll take a while, so I'll write a review now. Also for context, I've already played Ryza 1 too.

## Early Game

Camera mode is still way too good from Ryza 1. Not exaggerating to say 80% of this game's price is for this mode ~~or maybe not~~. So cute.

{{<cldimg url="https://res.cloudinary.com/takenoko/image/upload/v1680258537/daily/games/atelier-ryza2/%E5%8F%AF%E6%84%9B%E3%81%84_1_socao9.webp" alt="Heroines">}}

The thumbnail of this page was also taken in camera mode, and the vibe is god-tier, my personal best picture. Anyway, after enjoying camera mode for around 2 hours, I finally started progressing the story.

Main improvements from Ryza 1 that I noticed early on:

1. Auto-advance for dialogue
2. Reduced map-to-map travel
3. More facial expressions and pose patterns in camera mode
4. Auto-save
5. Small steps are easier to jump over
6. ~~Somehow Romy-san got way cuter~~

First, auto dialogue is huge. Being able to run dialogue on AUTO with one button like below is great. In Ryza 1, there were already many cutscenes and the pacing dragged, plus you had to press a button for every line, which was painful. This time, auto-advance was finally added.

{{<cldimg url="https://res.cloudinary.com/takenoko/image/upload/v1680258537/daily/games/atelier-ryza2/%E4%BC%9A%E8%A9%B1%E8%87%AA%E5%8B%95%E3%82%B9%E3%82%AD%E3%83%83%E3%83%97_ow2jgj.webp" alt="Dialogue advance">}}

Also, I liked that map movement tied to story progression is now partially automatic. In the previous game, it often forced the player to move maps even when you were clearly done with that area and just wanted to return to town. This is a welcome improvement.

And it's nice that camera mode now lets you combine character expressions and poses much more freely. Thanks to that, players can capture many scenes with their favorite expressions.

Plus there are must-have modern RPG features like auto-save and easier obstacle traversal on maps, which reduced in-play stress a lot.

Huge thanks to the developers for improving Ryza 1, which felt like a stress-heavy "Showa/Heisei-era RPG," this much... **and thank you for improving character design/modeling of Romy-san and other NPCs too...;;**

Besides side stories, I also started raising reputation with many groups (thieves, whole town, students, etc.).

I took a lot of bulletin board jobs, but honestly, **I never fully understood what the benefit of raising reputation was** all the way to the end. I maxed all reputation except the annoying "nobility" one, but still didn't really get it w

Also I kept getting new items and spent a lot of time on alchemy. Since I had played alchemy quite deeply in Ryza 1, I enjoyed it here with relatively little stress. **If anything, the whole alchemy system felt improved and more fun than in Ryza 1.**

Also, it was a good balance adjustment that the broken weapon "Space-Time Astronomical Clock," which was super easy to make in Ryza 1, is no longer so easy to craft.

In Ryza 1, just making that clock could let you beat the final boss and it was easy to make, so many players probably felt game balance was kinda questionable.

## Overall Review

Overall, it's a genuinely good game that evolved properly from Ryza 1. Combat, alchemy, camera mode, and fine systems all improve areas that felt weak in Ryza 1 (story? never heard of her).

When it's on Steam sale, you can get it fairly cheap, so it may be an easy recommendation. At least, I recommend it much more strongly than Ryza 1.

That said, compared to AAA JRPG titles like FF7R, there are parts where it falls short, so I'd recommend it specifically as a game to enjoy characters, alchemy, and combat.

### Slightly Meh Points

As you progress, craftable items and gear unlock via the skill tree from early to mid game, but after late game this skill tree becomes super dead.

{{<cldimg url="https://res.cloudinary.com/takenoko/image/upload/v1680258537/daily/games/atelier-ryza2/%E3%82%B9%E3%82%AD%E3%83%AB%E3%83%84%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC_zuqgcg.webp" alt="Skill tree">}}

If you really grind, you'll end up with tens of thousands of surplus skill points. I felt it would be great if those could be used to strengthen other elements (for example core-drive related upgrades). Meanwhile, upgrading core-drive related things is such a pain that it's basically ascetic training, so I wanted that to be improved.
