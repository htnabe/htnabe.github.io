Honestly, the exam content was so terrible I didn't want to remember it, so I wasn't sure whether to keep a record of it — but I decided to leave it here in the hope that it might be useful for anyone considering going to the Hokkaido University Faculty of Engineering for graduate school.

## Why Hokkaido University?

There are several reasons, but the biggest one is **that there is a lab doing cutting-edge research in a field I'm interested in**.

If I could do that at Shimane University, I would have been fine staying at Shimane for grad school too.

## Required Knowledge and Fields for the Exam

The academic fields and general content required for the Hokkaido University Faculty of Engineering entrance exam are as follows:

{{< table "table" >}}

| Subject                                | Content                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mechanics of Materials                 | Tension, bending, torsion, **triaxial stress, strain energy (Castigliano's theorem)**, etc.                                                                                                          |
| Machine Dynamics & Control Engineering | Questions on the basics of control engineering and machine dynamics — if you're a Shimane student, you should be able to ace this with just review.                                                  |
| Thermodynamics                         | Cycle efficiency, moisture/dryness fraction, **applied problems on exergy**, etc.                                                                                                                    |
| Fluid Mechanics                        | Non-viscous/**viscous**/incompressible flow, **NS equation and boundary layer equations**, applications of Bernoulli's theorem, complex velocity potential applications, etc.                        |
| English                                | TOEIC or TOEFL scores required. I personally recommend TOEIC since the scoring conversion used by the Faculty of Engineering for TOEFL is unclear, and it feels easier to get a good score on TOEIC. |
{{</table>}}
The bold portions are concepts not covered at Shimane University. I studied these mostly independently over 3.5 months.

- April–May: Studying for TOEFL iBT (mainly online English conversation)
- May–July: Acquiring foundational knowledge by solving problems from the 4-subject problem collection
- August: Simulating the actual exam by working through past papers

## Exam Schedule

- 8/24: TOEFL ITP (available to those who want to take it)
- 8/25: Written exam
- 8/26: Interview (scheduled at 5 minutes per person on paper)

This year, TOEFL ITP was added as a last-minute measure to help students who couldn't score high enough in English due to COVID.

I didn't think I could score better than on the TOEFL iBT I took in May, but I decided to take the ITP too on the off chance.

TOEFL ITP isn't the main topic of this article so I'll skip the details, but **honestly it was pretty unlucky**.

## Written Exam

For the morning exam covering mechanics of materials, machine dynamics, and control engineering, **I forgot my scientific calculator**.

I left my calculator at the hotel on this critical day... (and I did this for subjects I'm actually good at)

That said, most of the problems could be solved without a calculator, so I don't think it had that big an impact on the results.

I had my calculator for the afternoon fluid mechanics/thermodynamics exam, but I probably only answered about 60% of the fluid problems and 30% of the thermodynamics problems correctly (which was the main reason I failed).

**Despair.**

## Interview

"With these written exam results, I've clearly failed (I was sure of it)," so going to the interview felt quite daunting.

For the interview, I was asked to answer the following:

1. Reason for applying
2. My graduation research (if I had started it)

Additional questions included:

- If accepted, will you definitely enroll?
- Have you already made contact with your desired professor?
- Are you currently job hunting or applying to other universities?
- What will you do if you don't pass Hokkaido University?
- How do you feel your written exam results were? Which subject was the hardest?
- How do you feel about the fact that the curriculum at Hokkaido University and Shimane University are completely different?

Given all that, **since I thought the interview time was too short to explain my graduation research, I prepared and handed over materials in advance**.

The interviewers seemed to accept these materials in place of a verbal explanation, so **I didn't need to explain my graduation research myself**.

Knowing in advance that the interview was only 5 minutes on paper, I was able to explain my reason for applying fairly concisely alongside my background.

By the way, I practiced answering any possible interview question beforehand. **This is genuinely important.**

Having experienced the ~~pressure~~ interview for Tobitate! Study Abroad Japan, I found the grad school interview much more relaxed. I probably got full marks on the interview.

Incidentally, the interview ran slightly over 5 minutes, about 7 minutes total.

## Lessons for Future Applicants

- **Contact your desired professor and lab early**

In my case, due to COVID, my planned study abroad was postponed indefinitely and I had sudden schedule changes, so I didn't contact the professor I wanted until May. If you're already planning to go to a domestic graduate school, I'd recommend getting in touch earlier.

- **Try to connect with current students at the school**

In my case, I was introduced to one grad student, but looking back now, I should have gathered more information about the exam (especially how to approach studying for it).

- **Avoid juggling two or three things at once as much as possible**

I think the main reason I failed was juggling three things at once over 3.5 months: research + coursework + exam prep. If you want to juggle two or three things, you should start preparing for the exam much earlier.

(Of course, being able to do that this time would have required predicting COVID.)

## Finally

Again, this is kind of insider talk, but since the curriculum between Shimane University Mechanical Engineering and Hokkaido University Mechanical Engineering is so different, Shimane students aiming for Hokkaido grad school will probably need to independently learn more than half of the material from scratch.

If any of you reading this are aiming for Hokkaido grad school, you need to understand that difference and build your own long-term study plan.

The costs associated with the entrance exam are not trivial, and there is relatively little you gain from the preparation alone. If you fail, you've essentially thrown that money away, so please understand that there is a real risk involved (I didn't understand this sufficiently myself).

I highly recommend thorough preparation. Wishing you all the best of luck.
