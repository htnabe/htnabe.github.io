I had my regular health checkup just last week, and I was happy to find out that I had lost about 2 kg compared to last year.

At last year's checkup in June, I was 78.0 kg and was even told I was in obesity level 1. I was so shocked that I started a small diet right away (no food restrictions at all, just trying to lose weight through exercise).

As a result, although I had gained around 10 kg from job-hunting stress and reached 78.0 kg again (with a peak around 82 kg), I managed to bring it down to 76.0 kg for now.

## Services I Use Regularly

I use the following services to track my workouts and weight.

- Workout: Strava / Garmin
- Weight: Withings

I use both Strava and Garmin on their free plans, but I can still collect enough data to create running graphs like this.

I originally focused more on cycling than running, so until January 2026 I only ran about 8 km per session at most. But after I started joining half marathons, I began doing runs of around 15 km per session as well.

I am running another half marathon next month, and I am excited about it :sparkles:

I also log my weight with `Withings`, where I can check trends like body weight and body fat percentage in the web app (weight on top, body fat below).

{{<cldimg url="https://res.cloudinary.com/takenoko/image/upload/v1780926022/daily/%E3%83%A9%E3%83%B3%E3%83%8B%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0/healthmate.withings.com_37544061_weight_graph_cmzsvv.png" alt="Weight on top, body fat below">}}

It is a little hard to read, but it looks like my weight peaked around last May (about 82-85 kg) and has gradually gone down since then.

This is a bit unrelated, but as an engineer, thinking about the background that led to such a hard-to-read graph made me a little sad. It feels like this feature probably was not treated as very important, and the vibe is strongly "we built this just to satisfy the minimum requirements."

Personally, I would really appreciate being able to see weight, body fat mass, body fat percentage, BMI, and similar metrics together in one easy-to-read view. Maybe this demand just is not that common.
