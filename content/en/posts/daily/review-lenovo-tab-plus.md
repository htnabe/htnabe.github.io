## :calendar: After 4 Months of Use

### Conclusion

If you treat it as "for entertainment consumption," I think it's one of the strongest options in this price range. Surprisingly, even heavy smartphone games run without major issues depending on settings.

On the other hand, the body weight is **about 650g, which is on the heavy side**. If you hold it with one hand on a commuter train, the corners can dig into your hand and your arm gets tired, so I can't really recommend it to people looking for a super-light device.

Battery life is **normal**. It depends on performance settings and frequency of use, but I charge mine about 2-3 times a week. Compared with typical smartphones/tablets, it doesn't feel dramatically different.

### :sound: Audio Experience

As many review sites and Lenovo itself emphasize, the commitment to sound quality feels very strong. With eight JBL speakers, it produces rich sound that doesn't feel like a tablet.

You might not notice much difference for YouTube talk videos, but when playing music on Spotify or Radiko, the immersion is incomparable to typical smartphones or laptops. Being able to get high-quality BGM with just this one device, without a separate Bluetooth speaker, is a big advantage :muscle:

### :tv: Satisfaction of a 2K Display

Given the real market price, having a 2K display (2000x1200) in this class is another strong point.

{{<cldimg url="https://res.cloudinary.com/takenoko/image/upload/v1764488012/daily/Lenovo%20Tab%20Plus/visual_sound_rgugkm.png" alt="Figure showing a beautiful image displayed on a tablet">}}

Recently there are many 2K/4K videos and high-quality images, so roughness can stand out on FHD. On this device, not only YouTube but also fine text in magazines and manga appears clearly, so for basic use cases like "watch videos" and "read books," I don't run into screen quality problems.

### Kickstand Is Surprisingly Useful

Personally, what felt "more useful than expected" was the built-in kickstand on the back.

With past tablets, it was common to "attach a heavy cover to make it stand," but Lenovo Tab Plus can stand by itself. And you can adjust it across a wide range from shallow to deep angles.

{{<cldimg url="https://res.cloudinary.com/takenoko/image/upload/v1764487464/daily/Lenovo%20Tab%20Plus/tablet-kadouiki_zyhsub.png" alt="Range of stand motion" caption="It can actually recline nearly 180 degrees">}}

- Show recipes in the kitchen
- Place it next to your desk for casual side viewing
- Lie down and set a slight angle

For use cases like these, being able to do it in a "lightweight, no-cover-needed state" is a big plus for me. The body itself is heavy, but since you can skip using a cover, total operating weight feels about even.

### :book: Reading Mode and Performance :video_game:

Personally I spend the most time reading on this tablet, and for "reading books," a nice subtle feature is the **reading mode** that can switch the screen to monochrome or chromatic (eye-friendly colors).

By the way, I also bought a pen for studying during commute, shown below. It lasts fine with once-a-week charging, so I'm very satisfied right now.

## Summary

Lenovo Tab Plus isn't "ultra high-spec," but if your goal is "comfortably enjoying video/audio and reading," I can normally recommend it.

- Want to enjoy videos/music/reading privately within a reasonable budget
- Want it to stand by itself without a heavy cover

If you have needs like these, it's definitely a "buy" option.
